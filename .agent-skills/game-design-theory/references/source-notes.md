# Game design theory source notes

## Primary and upstream sources

### MDA

- Hunicke, LeBlanc, and Zubek, "MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research"
- PDF: <https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf>
- Use: distinction and relationship among mechanics, dynamics, and aesthetics.
- Do not infer: universal aesthetics, numeric tuning rules, or a complete validation method.

### Self-determination theory in games

- Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski, "The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination
  Theory Approach"
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8>
- Use: autonomy, competence, and relatedness as contextual motivation hypotheses.
- Do not infer: a mandatory feature checklist or player classification.

### Bartle player types

- Richard Bartle, "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs"
- Author-hosted text: <https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm>
- Use: historical, domain-bounded MUD player-interest model when relevant.
- Do not infer: universal or immutable modern game segments.

### Dark-pattern evidence boundary

- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light"
- Report page: <https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light>
- Use: recognize interface/design practices that can subvert or impair user choice.
- Do not infer: jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

## Claim policy

- Frameworks organize hypotheses; they do not certify that a design works.
- A causal arrow stays an assumption until supported by observed or measured evidence.
- Numeric sample sizes, ratios, progression curves, reward intervals, and target metrics belong
  to the project's risk and population model.
- Player reports, telemetry, implementation facts, and designer interpretations remain
  separate evidence types.
- A result can be inconclusive without forcing a keep/reject conclusion.
